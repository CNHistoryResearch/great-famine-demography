# Methodologies behind the published estimates of excess deaths in China's Great Leap Forward famine (1958–1962), and the scholarly disputes between them

Scope note: these notes cover method and critique only. Another researcher is covering the dated chronology of first appearances and the censorship history. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED]** could not be confirmed from a primary or reliable secondary source in this session. Items marked **[SECONDARY]** come from a secondary summary rather than the original publication.

## Q1. Structured entries for each major estimate (author, number, data, method, assumptions, criticisms, replication)

### Takeaway
The estimates fall into four families:
1. **Registration-based** (official crude death rates against a "normal" baseline): about 13–17M.
2. **Census-reconstruction** (1953/1964/1982 censuses plus the 1982 1-per-1000 fertility survey, which correct for under-registered deaths and births): about 17M (Jiang) to 30M (Ashton/Banister).
3. **Local-record summation** (county gazetteers, provincial archives, provincial statistics adjusted upward): about 32.5–45M+.
4. **Denial-side** (registration accounting that attributes the deficit to hukou/migration errors, or that inflates the baseline): about 2.5–5M.

Most of the gap between families comes from how each corrects for under-registered deaths, and whether it does so in the famine years and the baseline years alike. It does not mainly come from different raw data.

### Cited Findings

**A. Official-data-implied estimates (~15–16.5M)**
- **Official rates:** Official NBS crude death rate (CDR) was 10.80‰ in 1957 and 10.02‰ in 1962. Crude birth rate (CBR) was 34.03‰ in 1957 and 37.01‰ in 1962. — [NBS Yearbook 2000 table 4-2](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/Statisticaldata/yearlydata/YB2000e/D02E.htm)
- **Famine-year rates:** The official death rate rose from 11.98‰ (1958) to 25.43‰ (1960). The birth rate fell from 29.22‰ (1958) to 20.86‰ (1960). — [Wikipedia, Great Chinese Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) [SECONDARY]
- **Chen Shuo's baseline:** 1959 CDR was 14.59‰, 3.10‰ above the 1955–57 average of 11.49‰, and 1960 peaked at 25.43‰. — [Chen Shuo 2011, 世界经济 (CDT repost)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **1961 rate:** The official 1961 CDR of 14.24‰ is from memory and was not confirmed here. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Yang Jisheng's NBS calculation:** Using NBS data, Yang gets 16.1992M (1619.92万) non-normal deaths and 31.5M fewer births, a total loss of about 47.7M. He takes a "normal" CDR of 10.47‰ (the average of the three pre-famine years and the three post-famine years) and a "normal" CBR of 35.10‰. — [Yang Jisheng lecture, 2010 (CDT)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
- **Yang's provincial calculation:** The same formula applied to the summed provincial 《中国人口》 volumes gives 20.98M deaths and 32.2M fewer births. — [same](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
- **Cao Shuji's figure for official rates:** Cao lists a calculation from NBS death rates at about 15M (约1500万). — [Cao Shuji 2005, 中国人口科学 (CDT repost)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Smil's account:** The first American demographer estimates were 16.6–23M. Later, more detailed studies reached 23–30M. — [Smil 1999 BMJ, summarized by ENN](https://ennonline.net/fex/10/china)
- **Meng, Qian & Yared's attribution:** They attribute the low end of "16.5 million" to "Coale, 1981". — [Meng, Qian & Yared 2015, REStud (final draft)](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/qian/resources/MPQY_FINAL.pdf)
  - **Unresolved:** The "~16.5M" figure seems to be both the pre-1982-census early estimate and the official-data-implied number. I could not confirm whether "Coale 1981" really reported 16.5M. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **1984 NBS statement:** In 1984 an NBS spokesman (Xu Gang) said "over 10 million people died of unnatural death". — [1984 news wire text archived at SAILDART](https://www.saildart.org/FAMINE.NS[E84,JMC])

**B. Ashton, Hill, Piazza & Zeitz 1984 (PDR 10(4):613–645): ~30M excess deaths and ~33M lost or postponed births**
- **Headline:** About 30M premature deaths plus about 33M lost or postponed births. The same reconstruction is documented in Hill's World Bank paper. — [Hill 1988, World Bank Discussion Paper 22](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf); citation: [IHSN catalog](https://catalog.ihsn.org/citations/46705)
- **Data:**
  - 1953, 1964 and 1982 censuses
  - The 1982 1-per-1000 fertility survey
  - Registered births and deaths
  - 1982 census children-ever-born/surviving
  - — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf)
- **Method (intercensal deaths):** Total 1953–64 deaths = 1953 population + intercensal births − 1964 population. Births are projected from the 1982 survey's retrospective fertility rates. — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf)
- **Registration completeness:**
  - Child-death registration was "only about 30 percent complete".
  - Adult-death registration was about 80.5%.
  - Birth registration was about 80% complete for 1954–61, rising above 90% by 1963–64.
  - Model life tables: Coale-Demeny "East" for 1953–64.
  - Assumed sex ratio at birth: 106.
  - — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf)
- **Adjusted versus registered CDR:** Adjusted CDRs by mid-year were 24.4‰ (1958–59), 32.8‰ (1959–60), 29.5‰ (1960–61) and 21.2‰ (1961–62). Registered calendar-year rates were 12.0, 14.6, 25.4 and 14.2. Life expectancy fell "almost 10 years below the 1953-54 level in 1959-60". — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf)
- **Period:** Wikipedia lists the period as 1958–62. The paper title says 1958–61. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) [SECONDARY]
- **Replication:** Banister (~29.9M) and Calot (~28.5M) reach similar totals from overlapping data, so the result reproduces within the census-reconstruction family. — [Mi, Yang & Jia (IUSSP paper), table of prior estimates](https://iussp.org/sites/default/files/event_call_for_papers/A%20study%20of%20population%20change%20led%20by%20China’s%20great%20famine_.pdf)

**C. Coale 1984, *Rapid Population Change in China, 1952–1982* (National Academy Press): ~27M**
- **Headline:** 24.81M abnormal deaths for 1959–61 and 26.8M for 1958–63, with 30.683M fewer births. — [Mi, Yang & Jia (IUSSP), prior-estimates table](https://iussp.org/sites/default/files/event_call_for_papers/A%20study%20of%20population%20change%20led%20by%20China’s%20great%20famine_.pdf); Yang Jisheng gives the same 2481万 deaths and 3068.3万 fewer births — [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
- **Press coverage:** Contemporary press reported Coale's figure as "27 million deaths due to the Great Leap". — [SAILDART 1984 wire](https://www.saildart.org/FAMINE.NS[E84,JMC])
- **Method:** Coale's estimate is described as a "linear" (超线性死亡) method: deaths above a straight-line interpolated normal trend. — [Cao Shuji 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017) [SECONDARY]
- **Data:** Censuses and the 1982 fertility survey.
- **Criticism (Cao):** The sample data are unsuitable for studying the famine. — [Cao Shuji 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Criticism (Li Chengrui):** Li Chengrui (1997) revised Coale downward to about 22M. — [Cao Shuji 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Note:** I could not open the NAP chapter text ("Mortality in China", ch. 5). The exact baseline specification is not verified from the original. **[UNVERIFIED in original]**

**D. Banister 1987, *China's Changing Population* (Stanford): ~30M**
- **Headline:** 29.871M abnormal deaths and 31.195M fewer births. — [Yang Jisheng lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html); [Mi, Yang & Jia (IUSSP) table](https://iussp.org/sites/default/files/event_call_for_papers/A%20study%20of%20population%20change%20led%20by%20China’s%20great%20famine_.pdf)
- **Period and data:** 1958–61, using censuses, birth/death records and the fertility survey. — [Wikipedia table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) [SECONDARY]
- **Press coverage:** In 1984, as chief of the Census Bureau's China branch, Banister was already quoted at 30M. — [SAILDART](https://www.saildart.org/FAMINE.NS[E84,JMC])
- **Method:** Her method is broadly the same family as Ashton et al.: adjust registered vital rates upward for under-registration using census and survey consistency. **[UNVERIFIED in detail]**

**F. 蒋正华 Jiang Zhenghua & 李南 Li Nan (1986/1987/1988): ~17M**
- **Headline:** 16.97M (1697万) for 1958–63, from annual life tables with a parameter-estimation model. — [aboluowang summary](https://www.aboluowang.com/2024/0525/2058725.html) [low-quality secondary; consistent with others]
- **Range of results:** Three data sets average 17.11M, with a range of 16.5–17.9M. — [Cao Shuji 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **IUSSP listing:** 17M for 1958–63. — [IUSSP table](https://iussp.org/sites/default/files/event_call_for_papers/A%20study%20of%20population%20change%20led%20by%20China’s%20great%20famine_.pdf)
- **Commissioning:** The project was commissioned by the State Family Planning Commission. — [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
- **Status:** Scholars generally regard it as the lower bound of actual loss ("此估计值一般被学界认为是实际人口损失的下限"). — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **Criticisms (Cao):**
  - Cao criticizes Jiang for relying on official death rates.
  - He doubts Jiang's assumption of only about 20% death under-reporting in 1960.
  - — [Cao Shuji 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)

**G. Peng Xizhe 1987 (PDR 13(4)): ~23M**
- **Headline:** 23M. — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)

**J. 金辉 Jin Hui 1993 (《社会》1993 nos. 4–5): 27.9–40.6M** (not in the brief, but methodologically important)
- **Method:**
  - Balancing equation: births − normal deaths + change in total population = non-normal deaths.
  - Normal CDR = mean of 1956, 1957, 1962 and 1963 = 10.57‰.
  - The 1964 census total (704.99M) is back-projected with published growth rates.
  - He shows that published population totals are inconsistent with published natural-increase rates.
- **Results:**
  - At least 27.91M for 1959–61.
  - 34.71M after a rural correction.
  - Possibly 40.6M.
- — [Jin Hui (aisixiang)](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11995.html)
- **Criticism:** Cao says Jin mishandled the urban population. — [Cao 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)

**K. 曹树基 Cao Shuji 2005 (中国人口科学 2005(1); book 《大饥荒》 Hong Kong 2005): 32.458M for 1959–61**
- **Headline:** "全国非正常死亡人口合计约3245.8万" (abstract rounds to 3250万). — [Cao 2005 (CDT repost)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Data and units:**
  - County gazetteers (地方志) for more than 1,000 counties, aggregated to Qing prefectures (府) so that 1953–82 boundary changes cancel out.
  - Population bases anchored to the 1953 and 1964 censuses.
  - — [Cao 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Method:**
  - Pre- and post-famine growth rates are used to project the 1958 and 1961 populations; their difference is the net loss.
  - Non-normal deaths = net decrease + (births − normal deaths) + net migration.
  - This modifies Jin Hui's formula.
  - Normal death rate = 1957 or 1958 level before the famine, 1962 or 1963 after.
  - Cao cross-checks and edits gazetteer figures he judges inconsistent (e.g. Luzhou-fu counties).
  - — [Cao 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Regional results:** Anhui lost 18.4% of its pre-famine population and Sichuan 13.1%. — [Cao 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Criticisms:**
  - Gazetteer data are "probably subject to under-recording". — [Ó Gráda 2011 WP](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP11_03.pdf)
  - Cao himself admits gaps and imputation, e.g. missing 1961 data for Chao county. — [Cao 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
  - Bramall gives Sichuan 9.4M against Cao's 6.3M, which suggests Cao may be low for Sichuan. — [Bramall 2011 China Quarterly (SOAS repository)](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Replication:** Close to Jin Hui's rural-corrected 34.7M and to Ashton's ~30M, using largely different data (local records versus censuses and the fertility survey). That convergence is meaningful corroboration.

**L. 杨继绳 Yang Jisheng 2008 (《墓碑》; English *Tombstone* 2012): ~36M non-normal deaths, ~40M lost births, ~76M total population loss**
- **Formulas:**
  - Non-normal deaths in year t = (CDR_t − normal CDR) × mean population.
  - Lost births in year t = (normal CBR − CBR_t) × mean population.
  - Normal rates = average of the means of the 3 years before and the 3 years after (CDR 10.47‰, CBR 35.10‰).
  - — [Yang lecture 2010](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
- **Summary:** 36M is described as the gap between famine and normal death rates, and 40M as the birth gap, giving a population loss of about 76M. — [Chen Ziming (aisixiang)](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/24241.html)
- **Inputs:** The same formula on unadjusted NBS data gives only 16.2M, and on summed provincial data 21.0M. The 36M figure comes after Yang adds his judgment of concealed deaths from archives and testimony:
  - Wang Weizhi's 33–35M.
  - Former hukou office head Zhang Qingwu's "2000多万" (over 20 million).
  - The 1964 write-off of about 8M people from the registered total.
  - — [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html); [Yang's rebuttal of Sun, 2012 (CDT)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=252335)
  - I did not find the exact step from 21M to 36M in the sources I accessed. **[UNVERIFIED]**
- **Criticisms:**
  - Sun Jingxian lists about ten cases where *Tombstone* allegedly treats total deaths as famine deaths or misreads gazetteers (Xinyang 436,882 total deaths; Fengyang 60,245; Wuwei; Linxia). — [Sun Jingxian, 红旗文稿 (aisixiang)](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/71783.html)
  - Yang conceded one Linxia city/prefecture confusion and defended the other cases. — [Yang 2013 rebuttal (CDT)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=326690)
  - Garnaut reviews Yang and Dikötter together, focusing on "the problem of the representativeness of evidence". — [Garnaut 2013, China Information 27(2)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0920203X13485390)

**M. Frank Dikötter 2010 (*Mao's Great Famine*): "at least 45 million" premature deaths, 1958–62**
- **Claim:** "the records I studied suggest that the Great Leap Forward was responsible for at least 45 million deaths". Of these, "2 and 3 million… were tortured to death or summarily executed".
- **Sichuan figure:** He cites a 1962 Sichuan Public Security Bureau list of 10.6M deaths in the province for 1958–61.
- — [Dikötter, "Mao's Great Leap to Famine" (IHT op-ed)](https://www.frankdikotter.com/start-reading/maos-great-leap-to-famine.html)
- **Derivation:** He takes Cao's 32.5M and adds about 50%, based on discrepancies between archival reports and gazetteer data. — [Ó Gráda 2011 WP](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP11_03.pdf); Wemheuer describes it as adding 40–50% from county police reports. — [Wikipedia, Mao's Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao%27s_Great_Famine) [SECONDARY]
- **Critiques by Ó Gráda:**
  - "much hinges on what 'normal' mortality rates are assumed", and "the archives do not distinguish between normal and crisis mortality".
  - Dikötter's "implausibly low 10 per thousand" baseline contrasts with a probable post-revolution CDR of about 25‰. A fall to 10‰ would have "saved" more than 30M lives: "One can hardly have it both ways".
  - The figures for suicide (1–3M), beatings and torture (≥2.5M) and the gulag (≥3M) are "weakly-supported guesses".
  - — [Ó Gráda 2011 (UCD WP11/03; published PDR 37(1):191–202)](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP11_03.pdf)
- **Other critics:**
  - Wemheuer disputes the 2.5M "beaten to death" and 1–3M suicide claims.
  - Walder argues the high toll "cannot be reconciled with age-specific population data".
  - — [Wikipedia, Mao's Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao%27s_Great_Famine) (citing [Wemheuer, China Journal 66](https://doi.org/10.1086%2Ftcj.66.41262812))
  - Garnaut calls Dikötter's treatment of Yang's material "sloppily drawn or disingenuous". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao%27s_Great_Famine) citing [Garnaut 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177%2F0920203X13485390)
- **Bramall's points:**
  - Dikötter's figure restates Chen Yizi's claim.
  - Archival death reports were not free of political incentives: local officials could exaggerate deaths to avoid procurement quotas.
  - — [Bramall 2011](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Dikötter's replies:** "The New Archival History of China's Great Famine", China Information 27(3) 2013. He defends 45M by pointing to Yang (36M), Chen Yizi (43M) and Yu Xiguang (55M). — [HKU hub record](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/198272); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao%27s_Great_Famine)
- **Replication:** I found no independent demographic reconstruction that reproduces 45M.
- **Sichuan 10.6M:** This is a total-deaths count. Treating it as excess deaths would be a category error. That is my inference from Ó Gráda's point that archives do not separate normal from crisis mortality.

**Q. Newer academic work (2009–2017)**
- **Houser, Sands & Xiao 2009 (JEBO 69(2):148–159):**
  - Data: provincial demographic panel.
  - Method: Bayesian.

### Inferences
- **Two corrections drive everything.** Every estimate is some version of (actual deaths − counterfactual deaths), so the spread comes from two corrections: (a) how much famine-year deaths were under-registered, and (b) what counterfactual CDR to use.
  - Registration-only work (NBS-implied, Houser et al., Jiang to a degree) makes little correction for (a).
  - Census reconstructions make large corrections for (a) and also raise the baseline.
  - Local-record summations (Cao, Yang) use registration-style local rates but with local data that captured more famine deaths.
  - Dikötter combines archival totals with a low baseline.
- **Strongest methods.**
  - The census-reconstruction family (Ashton/Hill, Coale, Banister, Calot) works from complete population counts rather than self-reported deaths, and has been reproduced several times at 25–30M.
  - Cao's county-gazetteer reconstruction reaches a similar 32.5M from largely different data.
  - Dikötter's 45M and the 2.5–5M denial figures both have serious internal inconsistencies, and no independent reworking reproduces either.
- **Calibration in Mi & Jia.** Their implied "normal" CDR is about 16.5‰ (43.6M over 4 years and ~660M people), well above the official 10.8‰. Their lower excess estimate therefore partly reflects a higher, under-registration-corrected baseline. This shows that correcting only the baseline, or only the famine years, biases the result.

## Q2. What role does the "normal mortality" baseline play?

### Takeaway
On official registered rates, each 1‰ change in the assumed normal CDR shifts the 1958–61 excess by about 2.6M. Moving the baseline from 11.49‰ (1955–57 average) to 10.0‰ moves the official-data estimate from about 13M to about 17M. The bigger issue is consistency. If the famine-year rates are corrected for under-registration, the baseline must be corrected too; otherwise the estimate is inflated (Ó Gráda's critique of Dikötter's 10‰). If the baseline is inflated without a matching correction to famine years, the estimate is deflated (the Cheng/Zhan and Yang Songlin style).

### Cited Findings
- **Baselines used, by author:**
  - Yang Jisheng: 10.47‰ (average of 3 years before and 3 years after). — [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
  - Jin Hui: 10.57‰ (1956, 57, 62, 63). — [Jin Hui](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11995.html)
  - Cao Shuji: 1957/58 rate before, 1962/63 after, county by county. — [Cao](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
  - Chen Shuo: 1955–57 average of 11.49‰. — [Chen Shuo](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
  - Meng, Qian & Yared: average of 1953–58 and 1962–66. — [MQY](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/qian/resources/MPQY_FINAL.pdf)
  - Cheng & Zhan: 1955–57 average plus adjustment for 1953–64 under-reporting, giving 4–5M. — [人口研究 2017](https://rkyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/Y2017/V41/I2/97)
  - Yang Songlin: two choices of linear-trend baseline, giving 2.6–4M. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-1661-7_7)
- **Ó Gráda on Dikötter:** "much hinges on what 'normal' mortality rates are assumed". The archives "do not distinguish between normal and crisis mortality". Dikötter's 10‰ is "implausibly low" against a true CDR of about 25‰. — [Ó Gráda 2011](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP11_03.pdf)
- **Bramall:** There is "no unambiguous way" to calculate normal deaths or births. The counterfactual extra births would themselves have produced extra infant deaths, and the 1982 survey shows infant deaths were under-reported. — [Bramall 2011](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Mi & Jia's reconstructed baseline:** The modelled normal deaths for 1958–61 (43.39–43.85M) imply a corrected normal CDR around 16–17‰, against the registered 10.8‰. — [人口研究 2016](https://rkyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/Y2016/V40/I1/22)
- **Registered versus reconstructed famine-year CDR:** Hill reconstructs 32.8‰ for 1959–60 against a registered 14.6‰ (calendar 1959) and 25.4‰ (calendar 1960). — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf)

### Inferences
- **My sensitivity calculation.** Inputs: official CDRs of 11.98 (1958), 14.59 (1959), 25.43 (1960) and 14.24 (1961, unverified), with a mean population of about 662M. Results:

  | Baseline CDR | Excess deaths, 1958–61 |
  |---|---|
  | 11.49‰ | ≈ 13.4M |
  | 10.8‰ | ≈ 15.3M |
  | 10.47‰ | ≈ 16.1M (reproduces Yang's 16.2M NBS-based figure) |
  | 10.0‰ | ≈ 17.4M |

  For 1959–61 only, the range is about 13.1–16.1M. So the "~15–16.5M official-data" figure is a registration-based number with a pre- or post-famine baseline of about 10.5–10.8‰.
- **Census reconstructions.** These correct both the famine years and the baseline upward. Because famine deaths were under-registered far more (entire families dying, rations kept by not reporting deaths), the excess roughly doubles to 25–30M.
- **Two asymmetric errors:**
  - Dikötter: the famine years are effectively archival or "true" totals while the baseline is the registered 10‰.
  - Denial side: the baseline is raised for pre-1958 under-reporting (Cheng/Zhan add about 8M unreported 1953–57 deaths), but famine-year under-registration is not raised proportionately.
  - Each produces a mirror-image bias.
- **Period matters too.** Including 1958 and 1962 (or 1963) adds or subtracts a few million. Coale's estimate is 24.8M for 1959–61 but 26.8M for 1958–63. — [IUSSP table](https://iussp.org/sites/default/files/event_call_for_papers/A%20study%20of%20population%20change%20led%20by%20China’s%20great%20famine_.pdf)

## Q3. How do the 1953/1964 census undercount, birth under-registration and migration change results?

### Takeaway
The 1982 census and the 1982 1-per-1000 fertility survey were the hinge.
- **Census reconstructions:** Retrospective birth histories let demographers rebuild births for 1953–64. Deaths then become the residual between the 1953 and 1964 censuses.
- **Registration estimates:** Registration missed about 20% of births and most child deaths. Any registration-based estimate (official, Houser) therefore understates both deaths and lost births.
- **Gazetteer approach:** Cao anchors gazetteer series to the 1953 and 1964 censuses and handles boundaries by prefecture.
- **Migration:** Migration matters for provincial and county estimates (Cao includes a net-migration term). For national totals, internal migration nets out.

### Cited Findings
- **Hill's completeness estimates:**
  - Birth registration about 80% complete in 1954–61, over 90% by 1963–64.
  - Child-death registration about 30% complete; adult about 80.5%.
  - Intercensal deaths: 1953 population + births − 1964 population = 156.875M deaths for 1953–64.
  - — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf)
- **Census undercounts:** I found no source quantifying net undercount of the 1953 or 1964 census. Hill's reconstruction treats both as essentially complete. **[GAP]**
- **Cao's handling:**
  - Uses the 1953 and 1964 censuses as anchors.
  - Uses Qing prefectures so that 1953–82 county boundary changes cancel.
  - Includes net migration in the identity.
  - — [Cao 2005](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Jin Hui's method:**
  - Back-projects from the 1964 census total of 704.99M using published growth rates.
  - Shows that published year-end totals and published natural-increase rates cannot both be true.
  - — [Jin Hui](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11995.html)
- **1964 write-off:** About 8M people were written off the registered total in 1964 (accumulated unreported deaths cleared at once). Unreported deaths were "not corrected the following year but carried forward". — Wang Weizhi via [Yang's rebuttal (CDT)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=252335)
- **Lost births:**
  - Ashton/Hill: 33M.
  - Coale: 30.68M.
  - Banister: 31.2M.
  - Yang (NBS): 31.5M; Yang (provincial): 32.2M; Yang (own): 40M.
  - Zhao & Reimondos give a 15–30M literature range.
  - — [Hill 1988](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/492811468771874832/pdf/Demographic-Trends-in-China-from-1950-to-1982.pdf); [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html); [Zhao & Reimondos](https://shs.cairn.info/article/E_POPU_1202_0329?lang=en)
- **Fertility collapse:** CBR fell from 34.0 (1957) to 18.2 per 1,000 (1961), and TFR from 6.4 to 3.3. — [Zhao & Reimondos](https://shs.cairn.info/article/E_POPU_1202_0329?lang=en)
- **Survey bias:** The surveys sample only surviving women, so families wiped out are missing. This biases mortality downward. — [Zhao & Reimondos](https://shs.cairn.info/article/E_POPU_1202_0329?lang=en)
- **Cohort proxy:** MQY use 1959–61 cohort size in the 1990 census as a mortality proxy. It is immune to reporting manipulation but cannot capture elderly deaths. — [MQY](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/qian/resources/MPQY_FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Births are the hidden lever.**
  - In a census-residual design, more reconstructed births mean more residual deaths.
  - The 1982 survey raised births (registration about 80% complete), and so mechanically raised intercensal deaths.
  - Critics of high estimates (Li Chengrui's revision of Coale, Jiang) generally assume more complete registration, giving lower deaths.
  - This is why the Jiang (17M) versus Coale/Ashton (27–30M) gap is mainly about under-registration corrections.
  - Jiang's assumption of only about 20% under-registration of deaths in 1960 is the point Cao attacks.
- **Excess deaths versus population loss.** Lost-birth figures are of a similar size to excess deaths (30–40M). Totals such as Yang's 76M "population loss" are not death tolls, and conflating the two is a common error in popular citations.

### Gaps
- I found no source quantifying the 1953 or 1964 census net undercount, or a sensitivity analysis showing how a 1–2% undercount in either census would move the residual death figure.

## Q4. Sun Jingxian's household-registration argument: what is it, and what supports or contradicts it?

### Takeaway
Sun works purely from registered (hukou) year-end totals and registered births and deaths. He defines "abnormal population change" as (Δ registered population) − (registered births − registered deaths). He finds a 1960–64 anomaly of about 26.44M and attributes it to migration registration errors (double registration and its later cancellation, unregistered moves, "pocket hukou" during the 1961–63 urban downsizing 精简), not to unregistered deaths. He then estimates only 3.66M (2011) or under 2.5M "nutritional" deaths (2013).

Critics' central points are:
1. Under the grain-ration system a person without registration could not eat, so millions of unregistered survivors are implausible (Yang).
2. Unreported deaths were carried forward and written off in bulk (e.g. about 8M in 1964), which is exactly what produces such an "anomaly" (Wang Weizhi/Yang).
3. The census-based estimates (Coale, Ashton) never relied on annual registration totals, so Sun's critique does not touch them (my inference from Hill's method).

### Cited Findings
- **Publication and core claim:**
  - Sun Jingxian (mathematician; then Xuzhou Normal / Jiangsu Normal University) published a 14-page paper in 马克思主义研究 2011 no. 6 (a CASS journal), calling "tens of millions of abnormal deaths" a "重大谣言" (major rumor).
  - He uses NBS data, with abnormal change = (year-end pop_t − year-end pop_{t−1}) − (births_t − deaths_t).
  - He holds that abnormal change depends only on migration-registration errors (omissions, duplicates, false reports).
  - He attributes the early-1960s fall to people moving from cities to the countryside without updating registration.
  - — [Yang Jisheng's rebuttal as summarized on CDT, 2012](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=252335)
- **English restatement (CCPS 2016):**
  - "neither unreported deaths nor retroactively reported deaths have an impact on anomalous population change numbers".
  - The anomaly comes from duplicate migration registrations, cancellations, unrecorded and retroactively registered migrations.
  - "This left 30.1 million unaccounted for".
  - — [Sun 2016, CCPS 2(1):453–500](https://rpb115.nsysu.edu.tw/var/file/131/1131/img/2375/CCPS2(1)-Sun.pdf) (text layer partly garbled; numbers incompletely legible)
- **Later formulation (红旗文稿):**
  - A 1960–64 gap of about 26.44M (2644万) is "abnormal" change from migration misreporting and later correction.
  - Population figures come from one registration process, citing Li Chengrui.
  - Many urban migrants returned to rural hukou in the 1961–63 downsizing (精简), some keeping "口袋户口" (pocket hukou).
  - — [Sun, aisixiang/红旗文稿](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/71783.html)
- **Attack on the formula.** Sun calls Yang's "某年饿死人数＝该年年均人口数×（该年死亡率－正常死亡率）" mathematically unsound. In his Changde 1960 example the formula gives about 146,000 deaths against about 6,000 edema deaths in the gazetteer. — [Sun](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/71783.html)
  - Note: this compares excess deaths of all causes with a single recorded cause (edema), so the two are not like-for-like. That is my inference.
- **2013 figure:** under 2.5M "营养性死亡", publicized September 2013. The term was criticized as a euphemism. — [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2013/09/07/dont-say-millions-starved-during-chinas-great-famine-try-nutritional-death-instead/); [中国社科网 2014 piece via CDT](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/362424.html)
- **2011 figure:** 3.66M. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) [SECONDARY]
- **Rebuttal: data provenance and incentives.**
  - The official data came from the Ministry of Public Security hukou office.
  - Former office head Zhang Qingwu said no formal figure was compiled and estimated personally "2000多万".
  - Statistician Wang Weizhi estimated about 35M abnormal deaths for 1959–61.
  - Under rationing, families and teams had incentives not to report deaths.
  - Unreported deaths were "carried forward and written off in one batch later", e.g. about 8M removed in 1964.
  - — [CDT summary of Yang's 《脱离实际必然走向谬误》, 2012](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=252335)
- **Rebuttal: rationing.** A move required transferring grain and oil entitlements tied to hukou. Unregistered people could not obtain food, and Sun did not explain how millions of unregistered migrants ate. — [same](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=252335)
- **Yang's case-by-case rebuttal (2013):** He concedes Linxia city/prefecture confusion and recomputes Linxia prefecture at about 49,600 non-normal deaths (1960 CDR 55.08‰ against a 1957 normal of 12.36‰). He defends the Xinyang, Fengyang and Wuwei figures. — [Yang 2013 (CDT)](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=326690)
- **Allied denial-side work:**
  - Yang Songlin (2.6–4M) and Cheng/Zhan (4–5M) follow Sun's approach of correcting the baseline and treating registration anomalies as non-deaths. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-16-1661-7_7); [人口研究 2017](https://rkyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/Y2017/V41/I2/97)
  - Cheng/Zhan's work appeared in 人口研究, a mainstream demography journal.

### Inferences
- **Sun's identity is circular.**
  - Sun's identity holds only if registered deaths are complete.
  - An unreported death leaves a person on the books until a later bulk write-off. The write-off then shows up exactly as "abnormal population change", the residual Sun attributes to migration.
  - His Conclusion 1 (unreported deaths do not affect the anomaly) is true only if each unreported death is never written off. The ~8M 1964 write-off contradicts that.
- **Census reconstructions bypass registration.**
  - Sun's argument targets estimates built on registration totals (Jin Hui, Yang, the NBS-implied numbers).
  - Coale, Ashton/Hill, Banister and Calot work from census counts (1953, 1964, 1982) and retrospective births. Censuses enumerate people present regardless of hukou status.
  - A purely internal migration-registration error cannot create a national shortfall in the 1964 census or in the 1982 age structure.
  - That is an inference from Hill's method; I did not find a published paper making this point explicitly against Sun. **[inference]**
- **Age structure and cohort survival.** Zhao & Reimondos's cohort survival (survival to age 10 falling from 76% to 59%) and MQY's 1990-census cohort deficits come from survivors' own records and are independent of hukou bookkeeping. Both corroborate a mortality crisis far larger than 2.5–4M. Child deaths alone in six provinces imply large numbers. This is my inference, not a quantified rebuttal of Sun.

- I did not find a peer-reviewed demographic rebuttal of Sun that uses 1964/1982 census age structures directly (e.g. in 中国人口科学 or 人口研究). Names suggested in the brief (王维洛, 姜晶) could not be verified as having published such responses. **[GAP/UNVERIFIED]**

## Q5. Excess deaths vs. starvation deaths vs. "unnatural deaths" (非正常死亡) vs. population loss including lost births

### Takeaway
The terms are not interchangeable. Much of the apparent dispute comes from mixing them.
- **Excess, unnatural or "superlinear" deaths** (超线性死亡人口 / 非正常死亡) are deaths from all causes above a counterfactual. This is the measure behind Coale, Ashton, Cao, Yang's 36M and Dikötter's 45M.
- **Starvation, "nutritional" or edema deaths** are a narrower cause-specific subset (Sun's 2.5M; CAS 1989 "15M+ from malnutrition").
- **Population loss** adds lost or postponed births (Yang's 76M = 36M + 40M; Ashton's 30M + 33M).
- **Total deaths** include normal deaths (e.g. Xinyang's 436,882 total deaths, of which 155,698 were "normal"; Sichuan's 10.6M total deaths for 1958–61).

### Cited Findings
- **Excess deaths defined:** actual deaths minus deaths expected under normal conditions. — [Bramall 2011](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Yang's separate formulas:** one for non-normal deaths and one for lost births; total loss about 76M. — [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html); [Chen Ziming](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/24241.html)
- **Jiang/Li Chengrui terminology:** "超线性死亡人口" (deaths above a linear trend). — [Chen Ziming](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/24241.html); [Cao](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017)
- **Sun's terminology:** "营养性死亡" ("nutritional deaths"). Critics noted it is ambiguous and a euphemism. — [Global Voices](https://globalvoices.org/2013/09/07/dont-say-millions-starved-during-chinas-great-famine-try-nutritional-death-instead/)
- **CAS 1989:** "约1500万人" died of malnutrition (Jin Hui calls this conservative); Wikipedia lists it as "15+M deaths from malnutrition". — [Jin Hui](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11995.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine)
- **Total versus excess confusion:**
  - Sun charges Yang with treating total deaths as famine deaths (Xinyang 436,882 including 155,698 normal; Fengyang 60,245).
  - Yang replies that he did not, and cites about 1.05M non-normal deaths for Xinyang from an investigator.
  - — [Sun](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/71783.html); [Yang](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=326690)
- **Dikötter's Sichuan figure:** 10.6M "had died" in Sichuan in 1958–61, a total count. — [Dikötter op-ed](https://www.frankdikotter.com/start-reading/maos-great-leap-to-famine.html)
- **Bramall on Sichuan:** 9.4M (vs Cao 6.3M). — [Bramall](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Violence-related deaths:** Dikötter's 2–3M killed or tortured to death are a subset he counts within the 45M. Ó Gráda and Wemheuer call these weakly supported. — [Ó Gráda](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP11_03.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao%27s_Great_Famine)

### Inferences
- **Category conversions.** If 10.6M total Sichuan deaths for 1958–61 are converted to excess deaths using a population of about 70M and a normal CDR of about 10–15‰, roughly 3–4M are normal deaths. The excess would then be about 6.5–7.5M, close to Cao (6.3M) and below Bramall (9.4M). This rough calculation is mine and depends on the assumed Sichuan population. **[inference]**
- **Denial side.** Sun's "nutritional deaths" figure should not be compared like-for-like with all-cause excess estimates. Doing so mixes categories.

## Q6. Synthesis: what drives the spread (~15M to ~45M, and the denial-side ~2.5–5M), and which estimates are methodologically strongest?

### Takeaway
Four factors drive the spread, roughly in order of importance:
1. **Correction for death under-registration in famine years** (registration about 13–21M against census-corrected about 25–30M).
2. **The baseline.** Both its level (10–11.5‰ registered; 15–17‰ corrected) and whether it is corrected consistently with the famine years.
3. **The time window** (1959–61 against 1958–62/63).
4. **Data source and unit:** national census against county gazetteer against provincial archives; local-record approaches tend to go higher (32–36M).

Dikötter's 45M adds an ad hoc 40–50% uplift and pairs it with an implausibly low baseline. The denial figures raise the baseline and redefine registration gaps as migration.

Methodologically strongest are the census-reconstruction estimates (Ashton/Hill 1984, Coale 1984, Banister 1987, Calot), which cluster at 25–30M and are reproduced by several independent teams. Cao's county-based 32.5M is strong and independent but relies on gazetteer quality. Jiang's 17M and Mi & Jia's 16–23M are credible lower bounds that depend on more complete-registration assumptions. Dikötter's 45M and Sun's 2.5–3.7M are the least defensible.

### Cited Findings
- **Ó Gráda's specialist range:** 18–32.5M (Yao 1999, Peng 1987, Ashton 1984, Cao 2005). He describes Dikötter's 45M as "far beyond" it and calls for historians who "worry more about getting the numbers right". — [Ó Gráda 2011](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP11_03.pdf)
- **Jiang as lower bound:** Jiang's 17M is "generally regarded by scholars as the lower bound". — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **Walder:** high estimates "cannot be reconciled with age-specific population data". — [Wikipedia, Mao's Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao%27s_Great_Famine)
- **Bramall:** a settled toll "will never be agreed", but "we can still say much about its scale and its causes". — [Bramall 2011](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Mi & Jia's dependence on total deaths:** Their 16.24–23.37M range depends on whether Jiang's or Coale's total deaths are used. The estimate is driven more by total-death reconstruction than by the baseline model. — [人口研究 2016](https://rkyj.ruc.edu.cn/CN/Y2016/V40/I1/22)
- **Reproductions:**
  - Census-based: Ashton/Hill ~30M, Banister 29.9M, Calot 28.5M, Coale 26.8M, Luo Sheng 28.9M. — [IUSSP table](https://iussp.org/sites/default/files/event_call_for_papers/A%20study%20of%20population%20change%20led%20by%20China’s%20great%20famine_.pdf)
  - Local-record: Jin Hui 27.9–40.6M, Cao 32.5M, Yang 36M, Wang Weizhi 33–35M. — [Jin Hui](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11995.html); [Cao](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=98017); [Yang](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/128188.html)
- **Pre-1982 estimates:** The first American estimates (16.6–23M) rose to 23–30M once the 1982 census age data were available. — [Smil/ENN](https://ennonline.net/fex/10/china)

### Inferences
- **Ranking by method quality:**
  1. **Census reconstructions (25–30M).** Population-count anchors, explicit model-life-table assumptions, and independent replication by at least four teams. The main weakness is reliance on the accuracy of the 1982 retrospective fertility survey and the 1953/1964 census counts.
  2. **Cao 2005 (32.5M).** Different data (county gazetteers anchored to censuses) and fine spatial resolution. Weaknesses are gazetteer under-recording and gaps, and judgment-based edits. Its agreement with family 1 within about 10–20% is strong mutual corroboration.
  3. **Yang 2008 (36M).** The formula and baseline are transparent and sound, but the upward adjustment from 21M (provincial data) to 36M relies on judgment and testimony rather than an explicit model. Sun found some case-level errors, which Yang partly conceded.
  4. **Jiang 1986–88 (17M) and Li Chengrui 1997 (22M).** Technically sophisticated but conservative on under-registration. They are best read as lower bounds; they were produced inside state institutions and lean on official rates.
  5. **Dikötter (45M+).** No reproducible demographic method: a percentage uplift on Cao, archival totals that mix normal and excess deaths, a 10‰ baseline inconsistent with the archival totals, and weakly sourced violence subcategories.
  6. **Sun / Yang Songlin / Cheng-Zhan (2.5–5M).** Registration-only accounting that assumes complete death registration, reinterprets the bulk write-off of unreported deaths as migration error, and is contradicted by the rationing system, by census-based reconstructions that do not depend on hukou, and by survivor-based cohort evidence.
- **Defensible central range.** A reasonable reading of the methodological literature is about 25–35M excess deaths for 1958/59–61/62, with about 30M as the modal census-based figure. A further 30–40M births were lost or postponed; these are not deaths. Figures below about 15M or above about 40M require assumptions that the strongest methods reject.
