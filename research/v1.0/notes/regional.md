# Regional distribution of excess deaths in the Great Leap Forward famine (1958–1962): provinces, prefectures, counties, and where each figure first appeared

Companion tables: `regional/provinces.csv` (28 provincial units, with every verified cell filled; unverified cells left blank or marked NOT VERIFIED) and `regional/hotspots.csv` (35 prefecture/county/camp entries with their provenance).

Research limits: the fetcher could not reach zh.wikipedia, bannedbook.org (which has an English "Table 1 Death Rates of the Chinese Provinces, 1954-66" at https://www.bannedbook.org/books/famine/Research/fmtab123.htm), web.archive.org (blocked), the CUHK web archive (bot check), JSTOR (Peng 1987), or OpenEdition. Many province-by-year tables in the Chinese reposts I found were embedded as images and could not be read. As a result the **full 28-province CDR series for 1957–1963 is NOT verified here**. Only the 1960 rates for 8 provinces, plus Sichuan's series, are confirmed.

## 1. Official provincial crude death rates (CDRs), 1957–1963

### Takeaway
I verified the official 1960 CDRs for the eight worst-hit provinces: Anhui 68.58‰, Sichuan 53.97‰, Guizhou 52.33‰, Gansu 41.30‰, Qinghai 40.73‰, Henan 39.60‰, Hunan 29.42‰ and Shandong 23.60‰. I also verified 1960 natural-growth rates for 12 provinces with negative growth, and Sichuan's 1957–62 series. The rest of the 28-province table (from 全国各省、自治区、直辖市历史统计资料汇编 1949–1989 and the 《中国人口》 provincial volumes) is still a gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Official 1960 CDRs (‰), as quoted by Yang Jisheng** in his 21 Aug 2010 Yanshan lecture: Sichuan 53.97, Henan 39.60, Guizhou 52.33, Anhui 68.58, Shandong 23.60, Gansu 41.30, Qinghai 40.73, Hunan 29.42. The lecture was carried by Tencent Commentary on 27 Aug 2010 and reposted by CDT on 10 Sep 2010. — [CDT repost of Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=103671)
- **Sichuan series:** Yang gives five unlabeled values, 15.17; 46.9; 53.97; 29.42; 14.82, which are presumably 1958–1962. Bramall's Sichuan CDRs are 1957 = 12, 1958 = 25–26 (his text and table disagree), 1959 = 49, 1960 = 54, 1961 = 30, 1962 = 15. — [Yang lecture](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=103671); [Bramall, "Agency and Famine in China's Sichuan Province," China Quarterly 208 (2011)](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Anhui and Jiangsu:** Anhui's CDR rose from 12.3‰ in 1958 to 68.6‰ in 1960, and Jiangsu's from 9.4‰ to 18.4‰. — [Kasahara & Li, "The Causes of China's Great Famine… County-Level Evidence" (2017)](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f100904.pdf)
- **Anhui excess over baseline:** Chen Shuo puts Anhui's 1960 "non-famine" (excess) death rate at 56.87‰ (total 68.60‰ minus the 1955–57 average of 11.73‰). Liaoning's 1960 excess rate was only 2.23‰. Chen cites 《全国各省市自治区直辖市历史统计资料汇编》 as his source. — [Chen Shuo, 世界经济 2011(4), CDT excerpt](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **1960 natural growth rates (‰):** Anhui −57.23, Sichuan −42.23, Guizhou −32.4, Qinghai −27.66, Gansu −25.80, Henan −25.59, Guangxi −10.06, Hunan −9.93, Hubei −4.78, Shandong −4.10, Yunnan −2.07, Liaoning −0.3 (the Liaoning figure is for 1961). Source is the 《中国人口》 provincial volumes, as summarised from 《墓碑》. Sichuan had negative growth four years running (1958–61). Guizhou, Guangxi, Qinghai and Hunan had negative growth in both 1960 and 1961. — [陈子明 review of 《墓碑》, aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/24241.html)
- **Net population declines:** Sichuan lost 6,218,349 people over 1958–61 (93.94‰ of its 1960 population), from 《中国人口年鉴·1985》. The other declines Jin Hui gives are:
  - Anhui: 4.39M over 1959–61 (144.27‰)
  - Henan: 2,385,073 over 1960–61
  - Shandong: 2.34M over 1959–60
  - Hunan: 1.8397M over 1960–61
  - Guangxi: about 1M

  Jin Hui says 15 provinces together lost more than 21M. — [金辉《"三年自然灾害"备忘录》, 社会 1993 no. 4–5, aisixiang](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/11995.html)
- **Low-mortality metropolises:** Beijing, Shanghai and Tianjin averaged a CDR of 7.13‰ in the famine years, against 6.96‰ in normal years. — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **National series:** The national CDR was 11.49‰ on average in 1955–57, 14.59‰ in 1959 and 25.43‰ in 1960. — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)

### Inferences
- Hunan's 1960 CDR (29.42) is the same number as Sichuan's 1961 CDR in the same lecture. One of the two may be a transcription error and should be checked against the 《中国人口·湖南分册》.
- Yang's "15.17" for Sichuan 1958 conflicts with Bramall's 25–26. It is probably a typo for 25.17.
- In Anhui the 1960 emigration rate (about 55‰, per Ó Gráda, see §2) nearly matched the death rate. Population decline therefore overstates deaths in some provinces, and the reverse holds in receiving provinces such as Heilongjiang and Xinjiang.

### Gaps
- The full CDR table for 1957–63 for all 28 units is not verified, including the low-mortality provinces (Shanghai, Shaanxi, Heilongjiang, Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, Jilin, Zhejiang and others). bannedbook.org's "Table 1 Death Rates of the Chinese Provinces, 1954-66" exists (it showed up in search) but could not be fetched. The report writer should not insert numbers from memory.
- Tianjin's administrative status (my recollection is that it was merged into Hebei in 1958–67) is not verified here.

## 2. Excess deaths by province, by estimator, and why they differ

### Takeaway
Only Cao Shuji's 2005 provincial breakdown was found with absolute numbers. His top 13 provinces are: Sichuan 940万, Anhui 633万, Henan 294万, Hunan 249万, Shandong 181万, Guizhou 175万, Jiangsu 153万, Gansu 102万, Guangxi 93万, Yunnan 80万, Hubei 68万, Guangdong 66万 and Hebei 61万. Together these come to about 3,095万 of his 3,245.8万 national total. Peng (1987) and Dikötter provincial figures were not retrieved. Yang gives only round figures for Sichuan (>10M) and Anhui (5–6M), plus a sum of provincial official data of 2,098万.

- **Wan Li on Anhui:** Wan Li put Anhui's abnormal deaths at 3–4 million. This comes via Tian Jiyun on ifeng.com, 26 March 2013. — [en.wikipedia references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine)
- **Shandong Governor Zhao Jianmin:** He gave 65万 non-normal deaths from winter 1958 to the first half of 1960, plus 109万 people who left the province. — [陈子明](https://www.aisixiang.com/data/24241.html)
- **Bramall on Sichuan:** He gives Sichuan's excess deaths as 9.4M, set against "6.3M in Cao's work". The 6.3M is probably Cao's Anhui figure, garbled in the summary. — [Bramall 2011](https://soas-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/406222)
- **Chen Shuo on Sichuan:** He puts Sichuan's net loss at 1,150万, or 38.34% of his national net loss. His national range is 2,400–3,860万. — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **Becker (Hungry Ghosts, 1996), as relayed in a 2009 《纵览中国》 repost:** Henan 7.8M, Anhui 2.3M, Gansu 1.3M, Guizhou 1M, Sichuan 7–9M. The Henan figure looks inflated and should be checked against the book itself. — [CDT repost](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=110963)
- **Ó Gráda on concentration:** Sichuan and Anhui together accounted for nearly one excess death in two, but held only about one-sixth of the pre-famine population. — [Ó Gráda, UCD WP07/19](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/rest/bitstreams/1291/retrieve)

### Inferences
- Sichuan's estimates (Cao 9.4M; Becker 7–9M; "experts" ≈8M; Yang >10M; Liao Bokang 12M) vary by how much of the 6.2M recorded net decline is attributed to concealed deaths versus lost births. Sichuan's very low baseline in the 1953 and 1964 censuses also matters.
- Anhui's spread (2.3–6.3M) reflects three things:
  - heavy emigration, about 55‰;
  - large gaps between provincial and city/county gazetteers, which Cao exploits;
  - whether the source is "expert" back-projection from provincial vital statistics (≈2.6M) or reconstruction from county gazetteers (Cao 6.33M).

### Gaps
- **Peng Xizhe (1987), PDR 13(4):** The provincial table (Table 3/4, 1958–62, 14+ provinces) could not be accessed. Peng's national 23M figure is confirmed only secondhand.
- **Dikötter:** No provincial breakdown was retrieved.
- **Others:** 王维志 (provincial figures), 李成瑞 (provincial) and 冯客 are also missing.

## 5. Urban vs rural, grain exporters, and political radicalism

### Takeaway
Mortality was concentrated in rural areas. Grain-exporting Sichuan suffered most. Measures of political radicalism correlate with excess procurement and deaths. In Kung & Chen (2011), provinces whose first secretary was an alternate Central Committee member procured about 3 percentage points more grain, which they convert into about 1.11‰ higher excess mortality.

### Cited Findings
- **Urban vs rural:** In 1960 the non-famine (excess) death rate was 28‰ in rural areas and 13.73‰ in urban areas. — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **Sichuan as grain exporter:** Sichuan shipped out 15.5% of its 1959 grain output, compared with 4.50% for Zhejiang and 1.81% for Jiangsu. It supplied about 31.5% of all provincial grain exports. Its excess death rate was 18.09‰, against a national mean of 5.81‰ (Liaoning 2.8‰). — [Kung & Chen, APSR 105(1) 2011](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/sociology/abandoned-footnotes/2011-kung.pdf)
- **Kung & Chen's main result:** Provinces led by alternate members had an excess procurement ratio 2.82–3.88 percentage points higher. That equals about 17.62 kg of grain per person, roughly one month's consumption, or about +1.11‰ excess deaths. Against a mean excess death rate of 6.59‰ for 1959–61, political rank explains up to 16.83%. — [Kung & Chen](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/sociology/abandoned-footnotes/2011-kung.pdf)
- **Kung & Lin (2003):** A radical political climate significantly raised excess deaths, and once radicalism was controlled for, the canteen variable lost significance. **Lin & Yang (2000):** a higher urban population share and lower per-capita grain both raised mortality. **Li & Yang (2005):** resource extraction from agriculture explains about 33% of the grain-output decline, high procurement 28.3% and bad weather 12.9%. — [Chen Shuo 2011](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/?p=720876)
- **Yang Dali (1996):** His party-membership proxy (PARTY) has a negative coefficient on excess mortality, i.e. higher membership is associated with fewer deaths. The mess-hall proxy (MESSHALL) is small in Ó Gráda's re-estimation. In that re-estimation, log income and the grain-output decline (LNY, DAGQ) explain R² = 0.44 of the provincial variation. — [Ó Gráda](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/rest/bitstreams/1291/retrieve)
- **Kasahara & Li (county-level counterfactuals):** Grain exports account for about 12% of excess deaths, procurement rates about 60% and output decline about 46%. The shares are not additive. The export effect is larger in counties far from railways and in counties with fewer CCP members. — [Kasahara & Li 2017](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f100904.pdf)
